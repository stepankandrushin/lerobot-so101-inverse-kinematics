# How so101ik works

Read before changing src/. The numbers behind each choice are in measurements.md.

## The model

- src/model.c is generated by tools/gen_model.py from SO-ARM100's so101_new_calib.urdf and its STL
  meshes: each joint's origin (rotation and translation) and URDF limits, the tip frame
  (gripper_frame_link, between the jaw tips), every link's mass and centre of mass, and the meshes'
  surface points. Nothing is parsed at run time, so the same file builds on a board without a file
  system.
- Angles are LeRobot's calibrated degrees; URDF angle = calibrated + offset. The offsets are 0 by
  default, the URDF as it stands, as LeRobot's RobotKinematics takes it. An arm whose tip lands off
  can have its own: degrees per joint (so101_model.offsets in C; Model(offsets=...),
  Arm(offsets=...) or SO101_OFFSETS in Python). The gripper's 0-100 is the URDF's -10 to 100 degrees
  of jaw.
- The ranges are the calibration's (range_min..range_max around their middle, 360/4095 degrees a
  step): compiled in from a LeRobot calibration (SO101_DEFAULT_CAL), and set again from the arm's own
  file by the Python package at every start.
- Forward kinematics (src/kin.c): each child frame is parent x origin x
  Rz(q), 3x4 transforms. The gravity torque about a joint is 9.81 (a_x r_y - a_y r_x), r = sum of
  m c over the links beyond it less their mass times the axis's point, payload at the tip included.
  The cost is the sum of squared torques of lift, elbow, wrist flex and roll; the score adds
  comfort / 3 times the sum of (2 deg / range width)^2 over lift, elbow and wrist flex.
- The rules (so101_problems): pan, lift, elbow and wrist flex 5 degrees inside their
  ranges, the elbow, wrist and roll axes 3 cm above the table, the tip 5 mm above it, the roll axis
  and the tip out of the base (under 13 cm high and within 8.5 cm of the pan axis).

## The collision check

- The points: tools/gen_model.py samples each mesh with numpy's default_rng(0), 4 mm apart, 14960 points on the 7 links. A pose collides when a
  point of the upper arm or beyond is within the gap (8 mm) of the table, or a point of one link is
  within it of a point of another two or more links down the chain (the jaw and the wrist excepted,
  14 pairs).
- Each link's points are a tree of bounding balls (3113 nodes, leaves of up to 16 points), halved
  along the longest side. near_pair() walks two links' trees together: a pair of balls further apart
  than their radii and the gap is dropped, one within the gap of each other entirely counts as a hit,
  two leaves compare their points (each point first against the other leaf's ball). The table walks
  one tree. A leaf's points move into the world once a call (so101_world_pts, 180 KB), or every time
  with SO101_SMALL.

## The inverse kinematics (src/ik.c)

With the wrist roll fixed, a tip position leaves one degree of freedom, the gripper's pitch. Lift,
elbow and wrist flex turn about parallel horizontal axes (to 1e-5 rad in the URDF), the pan about a
vertical one.

- **The plane.** For a wrist roll and gripper, planar_prepare() takes the arm at pan 0 and zero URDF
  angles from the full model: the lift axis's place, the lift-elbow and elbow-wrist segments, the
  wrist-tip vector, the tip's lateral offset from the arm's plane, the gripper axis's angle, the
  moments of the links beyond each flex axis about it, and the roll axis. Kept until the roll,
  gripper, payload, offsets or ranges change.
- **A pose in closed form** (planar_pose): the pan from the tip's direction and lateral offset (once
  per target), the wrist flex's absolute angle from the pitch, the wrist's place, and lift and elbow
  as a two-link arm to it, for elbow branch +1 or -1. Points, torques, score and rules in the plane:
  one sine and cosine, two atan2 (so101_atan2, Cephes' approximant, 1e-16:
  https://github.com/jeremybarnes/cephes/blob/master/cmath/atan.c) and two square roots. A range end
  passed sets a second bit for which end, so two poses past opposite ends differ.
- **Newton** on the full model (polish) finishes a pose: x, y, z and pitch against pan, lift, elbow
  and wrist flex, the Jacobian analytic, to 1e-7 mm and degrees (2e-3 in float).
- **The curves in closed form** (find_loops). |wrist - lift axis|^2 is K - 2 M cos(psi) in the wrist
  flex's angle, so the pitches at which the two links reach the wrist are one or two intervals. On
  each, the pitch rises on branch +1 to where the elbow is straight (or folded) and falls back on
  -1: a loop. Where they reach it at every pitch, each branch is a loop of its own.
- **so101_ik** samples each loop every pitch_step degrees (5), and halves a step (up to 5 times) where
  the two samples break different rules or a joint moves more than 20 degrees: an allowed stretch
  narrower than a step lies between samples that break different rules. From every local minimum of
  the allowed samples it goes downhill in the plane to 1e-4 degrees, and the best of these, if the
  full model passes it (rules and meshes), is the answer. If it collides: out of the colliding
  stretch in 0.25-degree steps each way to the edge of what is allowed, and downhill from there with
  every check, and the other minima; the best that passes. Then the best sample that passes and
  downhill from it. The plane's ranges are 0.001
  degrees narrower than the full model's, so a refined pose on a range end does not fail by rounding.
- **so101_ik_near**, the next pose of a path: the same, with smooth (0.0006 N^2 m^2 per degree^2) for
  each joint's change from the pose before, the search starting within near_window (10) degrees of
  its pitch, on both branches.
- **so101_ik_pitch**: the closed form and Newton at one pitch, near's branch first.
- Not reentrant: the samples and the plane are kept in static memory and in the model.

## The local solver: LeRobot's call (so101_ik_local)

LeRobot's RobotKinematics.inverse_kinematics takes the joints the arm has and a 4x4 target with
weights for position and orientation, and runs placo's QP steps from there. so101_ik_local minimises the
same thing, wp |position error|^2 + wo |log3(R_target R^T)|^2, in URDF degrees inside the URDF's
limits, by Levenberg-Marquardt: the Jacobian analytic (a x (p - o), a), a joint that a step would
take past a limit held there and the rest solved again, a step kept only if it lowers the cost (else
the damping goes up tenfold), at most iters steps, stopping under 1e-9 rad. Its fallback: when the
position is more than 1 mm off afterwards, the closed-form pose at the arm's pitch (at the target
frame's pitch when an orientation is weighted, then the steps again from there), kept if closer.
Python's RobotKinematics in so101ik/__init__.py passes a stock model (no offsets, the URDF's
limits, no table or meshes) as the fallback, so it answers as LeRobot's does where LeRobot's
converges.

## The arm (src/arm.c, src/sts.c)

The calibration is checked against the servos' EEPROM (Homing_Offset, Min and Max_Position_Limit),
Acceleration 254 and Torque_Limit at most 500 written to RAM at torque-on, goals set to the present
positions, nothing allowed to move 30 steps; goals every 20 ms (tick_us) on a minimum-jerk profile,
sag-compensated (3.7 degrees per N m of model torque); a stall (7 degrees behind with under 0.5 of
progress in 0.3 s) or an overload (45 % for 0.3 s) or hal.stop() freezes every goal where its joint
stands. move_to goes straight in joint space after the joint path's check (30 points, ranges a
degree out, axes and tip above the table, nothing below it); line_to and move_by go through
so101_ik_near's pose every 2 mm, refused if two neighbours are more than 3 degrees apart. unfold and
home (wrist up first; the start pose with the wrist straight, then the start pose, torque off;
straight up first when the tip is within 5 cm of the table). The start pose is the arm's own: where it
stood at torque-on if it stood folded there (lift within 10 degrees of 2 inside its range's back end,
elbow within 10 of 3 inside its folded end), else that fold from the ranges with the wrist flexed
76.5 degrees down, and roll and gripper as they were. A LeRobot calibration's ranges end where the
joints were moved to, their stops, so the fold follows each arm's own calibration.

- One sync read a tick gets position, load and current of all six servos (registers 56 to 70, 2.2
  ms on an M1 Mac). The registers and sign bits are LeRobot's
  (lerobot/motors/feetech/tables.py): position sign at bit 15, load at bit 10, Homing_Offset at 11;
  load in tenths of a percent, current in 6.5 mA steps. Degrees are LeRobot's: (raw - mid) * 360 /
  4095; the gripper 0-100 over its range.
- Ticks run on fixed deadlines (start + k tick), so a late wake-up does not push the rest back.
- so101_hal is the board: write, read with a timeout, drain, a microsecond clock, sleep, and stop, log
  and tick for the caller. src/hal_posix.c is macOS and Linux (1 Mbaud by IOSSIOSPEED on macOS,
  IOSSDATALAT 1 microsecond); the Python binding adds stop (PyErr_CheckSignals, so Ctrl-C freezes
  the move), log and the CSV.
- The joint path check uses no gap: two parts' surface points never come closer than 0, so it
  catches the table only.

## Fast runs (src/path.c, src/fast.c, python/so101ik/fast.py)

A closed path of the tip is planned whole before the arm moves; the arm then only replays numbers. The
limits and the servo's response are measurements (measurements.md, Fast runs), not the servos' data.

- **Poses along the path** (so101_path_poses): points 2 mm apart, each pose from the one before:
  so101_ik_pitch at a pitch given for each point, the elbow branch of the pose before first, or
  so101_ik_near for the least-loaded poses. Every pose allowed by the rules, the meshes `gap` (2 cm)
  apart every 5th point, no joint more than 3 degrees from its neighbour. A closed path must come back
  to its first pose (in the least-loaded poses a second lap starts from where the first ended, since
  the least-loaded pose at the first point need not be the one a path returns to).
- **The fastest pitch** (so101_path_fastest): the pitch is the same smooth function of the place along
  the lap on every lap, a mean and three harmonics. The best constant pitch from -75 to 75 degrees in
  steps of 15 starts it; then each coefficient in turn, up and down by 16 degrees, halving the step to 2
  when nothing gains, both elbow branches each time; the cost is the lap timed between two others.
  20-60 ms a figure on an M1. The gripper then nods with the figure: the wrist
  takes part of the tip's way, the shoulder, the slowest to swing, less. A local best, not a global one.
- **Timing** (so101_path_time): time-optimal path parameterisation on the grid of points. With x the
  squared path speed at a point, a joint's speed is its step times the root of x; its acceleration is
  the change of its step times x (the path bends) plus its step times half the change of x along the
  segment. Each point gets the largest x the speed limit and the bend at the acceleration limit allow,
  then a backward pass lowers x to what can still brake for what follows and a forward pass to what can
  be reached. The two parts of the acceleration are added by size, so the result is on the safe side of
  the exact optimum (TOPP-RA). A joint's straight move comes out as the trapezoid it should be (90
  degrees in d/v + v/a = 0.5558 s; make test checks it). A closed path gone round several laps is
  indexed modulo the lap, without copies.
- **Ticks** (so101_path_sample): the place along the path as a function of time, smoothed with a Hann
  window over 40 ms (the joints stay on the path; only the timing is smoothed, so no acceleration
  starts at once), sampled every dt (10 ms). The run starts and ends half a window early and late, so it
  starts and ends exactly at rest on its ends.
- **Driving** (so101_arm_play): at Torque_Limit 1000 and Acceleration 0 (so101_arm_fast) a joint
  follows a goal streamed at 100 Hz as a first-order lag of 60 ms behind a dead time of 22 ms. So the
  goal sent at time t is where the joint should be at t + dead plus lag times its speed there, plus the
  sag compensation of the slow moves (3.7 degrees per N m) and a learned correction, inside the ranges.
  The goal is worked out from the time each tick really starts, so a late tick sends its own time's
  goal. One sync read of position, load and current a tick; a joint err_max (12) degrees from where it
  should be for 0.15 s, any arm joint at 99 % load for 0.4 s, or hal.stop() freezes every goal where its
  joint stands.
- **Learning** (so101_path_learn): after a replay, each arm joint's error at each tick (where it should
  have been less where it was), smoothed by a Gaussian of 25 ms both ways in time so nothing shifts,
  through the inverse of the joint's response (the error dead later plus lag times its rate), 0.7 of
  it added to the corrections.
- **From the rest pose and back** (so101_unfold_path, so101_unfold_clear): one joint path, half a degree
  a point, timed like a run. Wrist flex, elbow, roll and gripper go evenly; lift and pan wait for the
  first quarter and catch up on a smooth step, because in the rest pose the gripper hangs just above
  the table, and the lift ten degrees forward with the wrist still down puts it there. The way is
  checked at 25 poses, each also with lift, elbow and wrist flex 6 degrees (tube) ahead or behind in all
  eight combinations, clipped to the way's ends, for any contact (gap 0); the replay stops at 6 degrees
  off. Backwards it is the way home.
- **In Python** (fast.py): closed_run plans and samples, join chains runs with straight joint moves
  between them, Arm.run (drive) takes the fast way out of the rest pose when the arm still stands
  there and the way is clear (else the slow unfold and a move at 60 degrees/s), replays with learning,
  and leaves the servos at the run's settings; Arm's way home folds at the run's pace (fold_home) when
  the arm still stands where the run ended, else puts the settings back and goes home the slow way.
